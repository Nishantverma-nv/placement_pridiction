M4 · Unsupervised learning

Clustering, structure & outliers

No labels here — finding segments, reducing dimensions and flagging anomalies in student profiles.

Clustering vs classification: supervised learning answers “will this student be placed?” given labels. Unsupervised learning answers “what structure exists that we did not ask about?” Both work on the same table; only the target column differs.

K-means — the modern Lloyd view

AssignEvery point joins the cluster of its nearest centroid — that is one Lloyd iteration.
UpdateEach centroid becomes the mean of its members. Repeat until nothing moves.
Initialise wellK-means++ picks each starting centroid with probability ∝ distance² — fewer dud starts.

Choosing k

  • Elbow method — plot within-cluster inertia vs k; take the knee where gains flatten.
  • Silhouette score — how similar a point is to its own cluster vs neighbours; ∈ (−1, 1], higher is better.
  • Domain check — does k = 3 mean something to a placement cell?

Mini-batch k-means samples small batches per iteration — near-identical results on millions of rows in a fraction of the time.

Hierarchical clustering

Agglomerative (bottom-up): each point starts as its own cluster, then the closest pairs merge until one remains. Read the dendrogram and cut at the height you want.

DBSCAN — density, and outliers for free

ε (eps) — neighbourhood radius. minPts — how many points make a core. Grow clusters from core points; reachability chains them together; anything left alone is labelled −1 = noise.

  • Finds arbitrarily shaped (non-convex) clusters — k-means cannot.
  • No need to pick k in advance.
  • Outliers fall out naturally — a free anomaly detector.
  • Sensitive to ε and to feature scaling (always standardise first).

Dimensionality reduction

PCA — production-style use

  • Projects onto the axes of max variance; components are linear, ordered, interpretable.
  • Always standardise first — otherwise the feature with the biggest units dominates variance and PC1 just means “large numbers”.
  • Use for compression, noise removal and as a pre-step for clustering.
  • Pick components so the cumulative explained variance clears 90–95%.

t-SNE & UMAP — visualisation only

  • t-SNE: perplexity ≈ local/global balance (5–50). Preserves neighbourhoods, not global layout.
  • UMAP: n_neighbors (local ↔ global) and min_dist (how tight clusters pack).
  • Distances in t-SNE/UMAP space are meaningless. Cluster size and distance between clusters are artefacts — read neighbourhoods only.

Anomaly detection

Isolation ForestRandomly split features; anomalies isolate in fewer splits. Fast, unsupervised, scale-friendly.
One-Class SVMLearns a boundary around “normal” points. Intuition level here — O(n²–n³) on large data.
AutoencodersNeural net compresses then reconstructs; high reconstruction error = anomaly. Deep dive in DLAC.

When to cluster, when to predict

Reach for unsupervised when…

  • you have no labels, or labels arrive months later;
  • you want segments for outreach (mentoring, resume workshops);
  • you need outlier screening before trusting the data;
  • you are exploring what features might exist.

Reach for supervised when…

  • a defined outcome exists — Placed / Not Placed;
  • you must measure quality (F1, AUC) and improve it;
  • the answer drives an individual decision.
The production trick: fit k-means, append each student's segment_id as one more feature, and let the M2/M3 classifier use group membership as extra signal — unsupervised structure feeding a supervised model.
